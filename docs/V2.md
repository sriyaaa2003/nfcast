# v2: ensembles, and capacity plans from a calibrated peak

Pure post-processing of cached forecasts (experiments/cache_forecasts.py, `cache_orch.py`, `ens_orch.py`, `orch_v2.py`, `orch_boot.py`), same test period and scoring as above.

**Do ensembles beat the best single model?** Test MASE, 95% interval resamples series:

| horizon | best single model | best ensemble | stacking (non-negative weights fitted on the calibration period) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 hour | Chronos-Bolt small 0.518 | mean of TTM, both Chronos, TimesFM **0.501** (0.456 to 0.546) | 0.502 |
| 6 hours | TTM **0.684** (0.645 to 0.728) | no mean ensemble is better (best: TTM + Chronos small 0.693) | 0.690 (weights: TTM 0.86, GBM 0.13) |

Averaging helps a little at one hour (3%, the intervals overlap) and does not help at six hours, where TTM is simply better than the others and
adding them dilutes it. Stacking learns to ignore the weaker models there. Conformal 80% intervals of the ensembles are as well calibrated as the single models' (coverage 0.83 to 0.84).

**Capacity planning with a calibrated peak.** v1 provisioned forecast peak x (1 + a hand-picked headroom). v2 records, on the calibration days, the ratio
true peak / forecast peak of every decision window and provisions forecast peak x the beta-quantile of that ratio, so beta is the only dial.
It also tries the upper (0.9) quantile forecast of Chronos-Bolt and TimesFM as the peak. The comparison is against the best of ten tuned reactive rules
(utilisation targets 0.5 to 0.95, chosen on the test period itself, which favours the baseline). Saving in cell-days at the same violation budget, 95% interval resamples the 12 cells:

| violation budget (steps) | TimesFM q90, calibrated | mean of the three foundation models, calibrated | Chronos q90, calibrated |
|---|---|---|---|
| 300 | -0.7% (-2.8 to 6.4) | 1.2% (-8.6 to 2.1) | 5.2% (-3.7 to 6.7) |
| 600 | **3.0% (0.6 to 5.2)** | -1.2% (-2.2 to 6.0) | **2.5% (0.7 to 4.0)** |
| 1000 | **3.2% (0.8 to 5.2)** | **4.0% (2.5 to 4.8)** | **2.7% (1.0 to 4.3)** |
| 1500 | 1.4% (-0.8 to 3.9) | **2.0% (1.1 to 3.0)** | 0.9% (-0.9 to 5.0) |

So at moderate violation budgets a forecast-driven plan saves about 3% to 4% of capacity over the best tuned reactive rule, and the interval excludes zero there;
at the tightest budgets the calibrated-peak plans show no reliable gain (one exception, not bootstrapped: Chronos q90 with a fixed headroom used 145.2 cell-days against 158.7 for the tuned reactive rule at 150 violation steps, 8.5% less), and point-forecast plans with the headroom grid of v1 do not beat the tuned rule. A perfect forecast saves 25.9%
(oracle: 107.8 cell-days against 145.5), so today's models capture roughly an eighth of the ceiling. One city, one 9-day test period, 12 cells.
